# Architecture

The UI thread owns the per-monitor DirectComposition overlays, the settings panel, the Manual, and the physics state. The portable core in `core.cpp` and `core.hpp` advances at a fixed 960 Hz. The 768-pixel lash has 49 rigid bars: one 67.584-pixel handle and 48 tail bars. A direct symmetric tridiagonal solve enforces their lengths. Hermite interpolation produces 65 drawing samples; these samples are not physical joints.

The user-provided model's heavy belly and light tip are preserved. Relative linear density is `0.04 + 0.96*(1-s)^2.4`; the end is 25 times lighter than the belly. Bending stiffness follows density squared. Over approximately one handle length after the grip, a smooth neck multiplier rises from 0.25 to 1. This makes the attachment region more flexible without changing the mass taper. Segment interiors may cross. No new self-collision or knot forces are added.

All lumped particle masses, including the handle, are doubled relative to the initial passive model. Bending stiffness stays fixed. The hand follows the cursor through an exact update of a critically damped filter with angular frequency 220/sqrt(2) rad/s. The wrist is a passive angular spring with a fixed downward rest angle, frequency 8/sqrt(2) rad/s, and critical damping. It yields to the cable instead of chasing a velocity-derived powered angle. Translating the hand, gravity, and stored elastic energy drive the motion. Cable, handle and outline widths are doubled in all drawing profiles, with width-dependent raster and overlay padding.

Bending uses planar angular springs and dashpots, updated implicitly with their effective rotational inertia. The damping ratio rises from 0.45 in the belly to 1.10 near the tip, reducing rapid local oscillations while retaining broad loop motion. This follows the effective-mass spring/damper approach described by [Catto](https://box2d.org/files/ErinCatto_SoftConstraints_GDC2011.pdf). Air resistance remains quadratic and anisotropic, stronger across the cable than along it, with increased resistance at very high transverse speed.

The velocity constraint solve is a pure mass-weighted projection. Artificial transverse buckling kicks are removed. A final velocity projection follows the positional corrections so that both bar lengths and tangent velocities satisfy the constraints, following the position/velocity separation in [RATTLE](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991(83)90014-1). The overall model is a planar constrained bar chain; it is not a full three-dimensional elastic-rod implementation. [Discrete Elastic Rods](https://www.lmm.jussieu.fr/~audoly/publi/BergouWardetzkyRobinson-Discrete%20Elastic%20Rods-2008-00.pdf) provides the broader energy-based rod formulation. Derivations and numerical comparisons are documented in [physics](PHYSICS.md).

Control movement is interpolated across fixed physics steps. The active frame timer runs approximately every 7 ms; simulation accumulates fixed steps independently of rendering. A settled whip stops scheduling frames. Each monitor has a premultiplied BGRA swap chain starting at 512 × 512, growing in 64-pixel increments to enclose the whip. The panel uses a flat dark surface, restrained controls, and native rounded corners. The selected C lash is drawn as one closed Bezier contour with a 55.25% wider handle and a seamless width transition. A cached bitmap target renders it on a three-pixel grid with nearest-neighbor enlargement; physics remains continuous.

The input thread owns WH_MOUSE_LL and WH_KEYBOARD_LL. The mouse callback writes physical points, original Windows event timestamps, and button state to a bounded SPSC queue. The UI consumes the movement history in time order; duplicate timestamps are coalesced. Overflow or input older than 50 ms clears recognition and pending contact. Injected events and the application's marker are excluded. A full-screen pause removes both hooks; background Raw Input observes Escape and F8 without suppressing them.

Recognition requires at least 28 ms of coherent fast motion, a strong peak (3000 px/s), acceleration, and sustained displacement (2500 px/s over a 100 ms window). This prevents a single delayed position update from becoming a strike. A sharp stop is confirmed after a quiet interval. Reversals, old input, buttons, slow deceleration, and renewed motion cancel recognition or pending contact.

ContactValidator examines the simulated tip after a confirmed strong stop. It requires reach of at least 80% of the total length, forward displacement of at least 30% of that length, and outward velocity above 2200 px/s in the stroke direction. The user-provided contact window is 180 ms after confirmation. Physical rollout can take roughly 100–180 ms, so the previous 80 ms deadline cannot be promised for this model. A folded or trailing tip produces no contact. Rest lengths remain fixed and the click uses the actual contact point; the validator does not push the tip toward an impact radius.

Exact and Sadomaso use identical physical simulation. Exact moves the held control point along the visible curve with a rigid coordinate translation, preserving all relative geometry and velocities. At contact its tip is at the captured mouse stop point. The free-tip contact checks remain identical in both modes.

Audio runs on a separate COM/MTA thread with immutable PCM buffers and 24 preallocated voices. OnCriticalError only signals an event. The audio thread recreates the engine and voices, with a two-second retry interval when no output exists. No missed impacts are replayed. Windows' virtual audio client follows the default output device; a device notification also requests recovery. Stopping unused audio processing reduces idle load.

Impact sound starts with a validated contact and a successful click. Invalid candidates do not pre-play an impact. Device buffering still contributes acoustic latency; synchronization requires loopback/camera measurements.

The main process sends a heartbeat to a guard instance of the same executable. The guard restores cursor visibility on process death or a four-second unresponsive interval. Session, power, display, and shell restarts have explicit restoration paths. The cursor scheme is not replaced.

The UI and settings use an English/Russian enum. English is the default. Mode, language, and portable-file precedence are independent of manual, UI, session, and full-screen pause reasons.

FlyoutView draws the 320 × 344 DIP settings card and the taller Manual with Direct2D and DirectWrite. Manual contains seven measured text blocks, using one 19.5 DIP body style, with inline semibold mode names and hotkeys, and both final notes in semibold. The same DirectWrite spans are used for measuring and painting. No quote bars, inset cards, book icons, or keycap illustrations are used. No native EDIT control or scrolling is used. Layout is fitted before the window is shown, keeping the full text inside the monitor work area. The target icon uses a native localized tooltip and opens the Training website through ShellExecute.

Manual pause cancels queued impacts and sound before starting a 640 ms visual coil. The user's F8 model is preserved: a rigid rolled ring hangs from the end of the handle as a pendulum. Its pivot follows a smoothed cursor; gravity and pivot acceleration drive its angle. The paused branch never runs gesture recognition or SendInput. Background mouse Raw Input wakes rendering when the cursor moves; the frame timer stops after the roll settles. Resume resets movement history and blends back to the live rope over 460 ms, suppressing gesture recognition during that transition. Full-screen, session, and UI pauses hide the overlay instead. F8 key latches and a short debounce prevent a hook-to-Raw-Input transition or key repeat from toggling pause twice.

The tray uses NOTIFYICON_VERSION_4 notifications consistently. NIN_SELECT and NIN_KEYSELECT request an idempotent show operation; legacy WM_LBUTTONUP no longer toggles the same window a second time. Deactivation is checked after the activation handoff, and a context-menu pause is retained while focus changes. See Microsoft's [notification callback documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/shellapi/nf-shellapi-shell_notifyiconw).

The sound pack retains one impact and two alternating whooshes. Processing uses an audible whoosh onset gate with 4 ms pre-roll, a -17 dBFS RMS target, and -2 dBFS peak ceiling. Runtime whoosh gain is raised to remain audible beside the impact. Cancelling a predicted contact stops only its impact voice; a real pause stops all voices. Audio diagnostics count successful submissions and observed sample advancement for each embedded buffer.

Windows input timestamps use the message clock, with 32-bit wrap extended against the current boot tick count. See [MSLLHOOKSTRUCT](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/ns-winuser-msllhookstruct) and [message time](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/nf-winuser-getmessagetime). Physics and contact scheduling use the high-resolution application clock; a confirmed stop is stamped on that clock when consumed.

The physics replay harness builds only portable C++20 and drives synthetic cursor paths. `python tools/test_physics.py --check` compiles a hashable source snapshot and writes metrics and traces without starting the app. Linux builds use the same core through `cmake -S . -B build/linux -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DWHIP_WITH_AUDIO=OFF`, followed by a normal build and CTest run. Numerical tests check the model; desktop animation previews are optional and separate.
